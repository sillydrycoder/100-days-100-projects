## Section 2.1: Acceleration and Velocity-Time Graphs
Acceleration measures the rate of change of velocity over time. On a velocity-time (v-t) graph, the slope of the line segment represents constant acceleration, while the area trapped between the velocity curve and the horizontal time axis represents total displacement. Students frequently confuse position-time slope (velocity) with velocity-time slope (acceleration).

$$
a = \frac{v_f - v_i}{\Delta t}, \qquad \Delta x = v_i t + \frac{1}{2} a t^2 = \frac{v_i + v_f}{2} \cdot \Delta t
$$

| Graph Feature | Position-Time (x-t) | Velocity-Time (v-t) | Acceleration-Time (a-t) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Slope | Instantaneous Velocity (m/s) | Acceleration (m/s^2) | Jerk (m/s^3) |
| Area Under Curve | Not physically standard | Displacement (Delta x in m) | Change in Velocity (Delta v) |
| Horizontal Flat Line | Object at rest (v = 0) | Constant velocity (a = 0) | Constant acceleration |

## Section 3.2: Forces on a Frictionless Incline
When a block of mass m rests on a frictionless ramp tilted at angle theta above the horizontal, gravitational weight mg is resolved into two orthogonal components aligned with the ramp surface: the parallel driving component pointing down the slope and the perpendicular component balanced by the normal force N.

$$
F_{\parallel} = m g \sin(\theta), \qquad N = F_{\perp} = m g \cos(\theta), \qquad a_{\text{ramp}} = g \sin(\theta)
$$

| Angle (theta) | sin(theta) | cos(theta) | Acceleration on Frictionless Ramp (g = 9.8 m/s^2) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 0 deg (flat) | 0.000 | 1.000 | 0.00 m/s^2 |
| 30 deg | 0.500 | 0.866 | 4.90 m/s^2 |
| 45 deg | 0.707 | 0.707 | 6.93 m/s^2 |
| 60 deg | 0.866 | 0.500 | 8.49 m/s^2 |

[diagram: Fig 3.2 | Free-Body Diagram on 30-Degree Incline | Block of mass 4 kg on a 30-degree frictionless ramp. Weight vector mg = 39.2 N points vertically downward; Normal force N = 33.95 N points perpendicular to surface; parallel component mg*sin(30) = 19.6 N points down the incline.]
